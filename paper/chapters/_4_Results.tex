\chapter{Results}

In total, the experiment produced 2700 observations across three construction strategies, three levels of coverage, and six particle counts.
Each configuration was evaluated on 50 independently generated datasets, with all three strategies receiving identical particle configuration.

\section{Bounding-Volume Checks}

Figure~\ref{fig:mean-bv-checks} shows the mean number of bounding-volume checks for the three construction strategies.


\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{bounding_volume_checks.png}
  \caption{Mean bounding-volume checks by particle count, coverage and strategies. - Generated using AI}
  \label{fig:mean-bv-checks}
\end{figure}

The number of checks increased with both particle count and coverage for every strategy.
Across all 18 combinations of these factors, the top-down construction produced the highest mean number of bounding-volume checks.
Bottom-up construction produced the lowest mean in 15 out of 18 combinations.
Incremental produced the lowest mean for 25 particles at coverages of 0.10 and 0.20, and for 50 particles at a coverage of 0.05.
In these three cases the difference between bottom-up and incremental was less than one check on average.

The difference between top-down and bottom-up was present across the full investigated range. 
Depending on the configuration, the top-down hierarchy required between ~56.6\% and ~84.0\% more checks on average.
At 25 particles and a coverage of 0.05 the means were 58.72 for top-down, 34.80 for bottom-up and 35.44 for incremental construction.
At the largest configuration of 1000 particles and coverage of 0.20, the means increased to 5,719.76, 3,306.28 and 3,922.48 checks. 

This difference was observed in almost all datasets. 
Top-down produced more bounding-volume checks than bottom-up in  896 of the 900 datasets, equal values in one dataset, and fewer checks in three datasets.
Compared with incremental construction, top-down produced more checks in 892 datasets, equal values in two and fewer checks in six datasets.
Bottom-up produced fewer checks than incremental construction in 601 datasets, equal values in 32 and more checks in 267 datasets.


\section{Primitive Checks}

For every individual dataset, the number of primitive checks was identical across all three construction strategies. 
Therefore, the three strategies are represented by a single curve in the Figure~\ref{fig:mean-primitive-checks}.

Instead, the number of primitive checks was determined by the scene parameters. 
It increased with particle count, and approximately doubled when the coverage was doubled.
For 25 particles the mean increased from 3.5 checks at a coverage of 0.05 to 6.48 at 0.1 and 13.62 for 0.2.
For 1000 particles the corresponding values were 128.12, 260.28 and 514.62 checks.

\section{Secondary Metrics}

Top-down produced the minimum tree height for every dataset, increasing from 5 at 25 particles to 10 at 1,000. 
Across the same range, the coverage-averaged bottom-up mean increased from 6.45 to 14.05 and incremental from 6.99 to 16.24 (Figure~\ref{fig:tree-height}).

\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{tree_height.png}
  \caption{Mean tree height by particle count, coverage and strategies. - Generated using AI}
  \label{fig:tree-height}
\end{figure}

When averaged across all coverage levels, bottom-up produced the smallest mean total internal AABB area through 200 particles.
At 500 and 1,000 particles, top-down produced the smallest mean (Figure~\ref{fig:total-aabb-area}).
