\chapter{Results}

In total, the experiment produced 2700 obervations across three construction strategies, three levels of coverage, and six particle counts.
Each configuration was evaluated on 50 independently generated datasets.
The same particle configurations and datasets were used for all three evaluated strategies.

\section{Bounding-Volume Checks}

Figure~\ref{fig:mean-bv-checks} shows the mean number of bounding-volume checks for the three construction strategies.


\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{bounding_volume_checks.png}
  \label{fig:mean-bv-checks}
\end{figure}

The number of checks increased with both particle count and coverage for every strategy.
Across all 18 combinations of these factors, the top-down construction produced the highest mean number of bounding-volume checks.
The bottom-up construction produced the lowest mean in 17 out of 18 combinations.
One exceeption occured for 25 particles and a coverage of 0.1 where the incremental mean was slightly lower than the the bottom-up (25.66 compared to 26.12).

The difference between top-down and bottom-up was present across the full inverstigated range. 
Depending on the configuration, the top-down hierarchy required between ~33\% and ~17\% more checks on average when compared to the bottom-up hierarchy.
At 25 particles and a coverage of 0.05 the means were 35.74 for top-down, 22.64 for bottom-up and 23.74 for incremental construction.
At the largest configuration of 1000 particles and coverage of 0.20, the means increased to 3541.2, 2310,24 and 2900.5 checks. 

This difference was observed in almost all datasets. 
Top-down produced more bounding-volumes checks than bottom-up in  885 of the 900 datsets, equal values in three datasets, fewer checks in 12 datasets.
Compared with incremental construction, top-down produced more checks in 863 datasets, equal values in two and fewer checks in 35 datasets.
Bottom-up produced fewer checks than incremental construction in 630 datasets, equal values in nine and more checks in 261.


\section{Primitive Checks}

For every individual dataset, the number of primitive checks was identical across all three construction strategies. 
Therefore the three strategies are represented by a single curve in the Figure~\ref{fig:mean-primitive-checks}.

Instead the number of primitive checks was determined by the scene parameters. 
It incresead with particle count, and approximately doubled when the coverage was doubled.
For 25 paticles the mean increased from 3.5 checks at a coverage of $0.05$ to 6.48 at $0.$1 and 13.62 for $0.2$.
For 1000 particles the correspending values were 128.12, 260.28 and 514.62 checks.
